# How In Russia earns its money

A plan, not a result. Every price below is an **assumption to be tested with real buyers**; the only sourced figure is the grant size in section 2. The plan follows the business thesis (`docs/memo/business_thesis_short_en.html`): free for the player, no advertising, no paid advantages.

## 1. Rules that fix the model

The players are newcomers who are already a target for fraud, and the game teaches them to spot it. So the money must never come from them or from their data.

- The player never pays for the story, the safety lessons, a character or an advantage. (The owner has since asked for income from play too: optional extras that change no outcome are planned in [REVENUE_PLAYERS.md](REVENUE_PLAYERS.md), which replaces the old "pays nothing, ever".)
- No advertising and no sponsored scenes. (The in-game adverts, billboards and shops use game roubles only.)
- No selling or sharing of data. The analytics stay privacy-first.
- No commission from loans, money transfers, "lawyers", agencies or any service the game points to. A scene that names a real service must not be paid for.
- Phone numbers, land and shops in the game are never sold for real money.

What is left is the people who **want newcomers to succeed and have a budget for it**: universities, adaptation centres, employers, schools, and funds.

## 2. Streams, in the order they can start

| # | Stream | Who pays | When | Size |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Grants | Funds | months 0 to 12 | "Student Startup" (Fund for Assistance to Innovation): up to ₽1 million; the 2026 call closed on 18 March 2026, so the next call must be watched for |
| 2 | Pilot, then licence | A university or adaptation centre | pilot month 3 to 6, licence from month 9 | assumed ₽100,000 pilot, ₽150,000 a year licence |
| 3 | Custom scenarios | Regions, institutions, employers | from month 6 | assumed ₽80,000 to ₽100,000 each (a short story pack with sources) |
| 4 | Employer onboarding | Companies that hire from abroad | from month 12 | assumed ₽250,000 a year (the pre-arrival stage and the worker characters, branded) |
| 5 | Voluntary support | Anyone | any time | small; a "gift a seat to a student" button, not a source to plan on |

**Grants.** Student Startup is the first step (the thesis already names it); a team member who is a student or a graduate student must apply. The "Start" programme of the same fund is the next step on its ladder, but I could not confirm its amount: check the fund's site before counting it. The Presidential Fund for Cultural Initiatives is for culture and art, so it probably does not fit; the Presidential Grants Fund (for non-profits) may, but needs a registered non-profit. Counted in the plan: only the ₽1 million.

**Licence, the main stream.** One price per organisation per year, with seats included, so a university needs no per-student bookkeeping. It buys: a branded start screen, a completion report per student (what was learned, where the mistakes were), the class mode, and an update when a law changes. Cheaper alternative if buyers prefer it: about ₽50 to ₽100 per foreign student a year (a university with 3,000 foreign students then pays ₽150,000 to ₽300,000, in the same range).

**Why the state helps the buyer.** Decree No. 309 sets a goal of at least 500 thousand foreign students by 2030 (422.2 thousand in 2025/26), so every university has a reason to spend on adaptation. A line for it in a university's budget is the thing to find out in the pilot.

## 3. Three scenarios for 24 months

Roubles, all unit prices assumed. Licences earned in the year, rounded.

| | Year 1 | Year 2 | 24 months |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cautious**: 1 pilot, 4 licences in year 2, 2 custom packs | grant 1,000,000 + pilot 100,000 = 1,100,000 | 4 x 150,000 + 2 x 80,000 = 760,000 | **1,860,000** |
| **Plan** (the thesis: 10 partners by month 24): 2 licences, then an average of 6 paying in year 2, 4 custom packs | 1,000,000 + 2 x 150,000 + 1 x 80,000 = 1,380,000 | 6 x 150,000 + 3 x 80,000 = 1,140,000 | **2,520,000** |
| **Good**: partners also pay for employer onboarding; a second grant | 1,380,000 | 10 x 250,000 + 5 x 100,000 + a second grant of 2,000,000 = 5,000,000 | **6,380,000** |

What this says plainly: at these prices the game **pays for its running costs and part of one person's time, not a team**. It becomes a company only if (a) a state or regional programme buys it for many institutions at once, or (b) one buyer takes the employer product at a larger price. Both are worth testing first, because they decide the whole plan.

## 4. Costs to put against it

Not yet priced; fill these in before any promise to a funder: a Russian server and database (and backups), a TURN relay for calls (needed before the public launch), the domain and e-mail sending, a lawyer's read of the legal texts, native review of the Uzbek, Tajik and Kyrgyz text, and a legal entity with an accountant. Running costs are low (a static game plus one small server); people are the real cost.

## 5. What to do in the first 90 days

1. Choose the legal form (sole entrepreneur, company, or a non-profit for the presidential grants). Licences and grants both need one, and so do invoices and closing documents.
2. Ask 5 international offices at universities for a 30-minute call and bring the pilot offer. Learn three things: who decides, which budget line pays, and what price they say yes to. Write the answers down; replace the assumptions in this file.
3. Prepare the Student Startup application for the next call.
4. Get one letter of intent for the pilot. Funders ask for it.
5. Build only what a buyer asked for. Likely first: a branded start screen and a downloadable completion report per student (check the class mode in the code first; it exists per the thesis, but the partner report and the branding are not built).

## 6. What would change the plan

- If three or more buyers say yes at a higher price than assumed, raise it and drop the cautious scenario.
- If buyers want per-student pricing, switch to it (section 2).
- If nobody pays within 6 months of the pilot, the grant line and a state programme become the only stream; stop building paid features and apply to funds.
- If a buyer asks for advertising or a paid advantage in return for money, the answer is no (section 1): it would end the reason players trust the game.

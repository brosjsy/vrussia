1. The game in short

V Russia is a free life simulator in the browser, set in Russia. The player picks a life (a citizen, a labour migrant, a foreign student) and lives two years of it: work, study, papers, family, culture, crime and the police, cooking, and many small everyday surprises. It runs on a computer or a phone, in English or Russian, offline or online with other players on the game's own server.

2. How a player plays

1. StartOpen the game, press New game, choose who you are and the difficulty, make your character. A foreigner can tick "start at home" to go through the visa and the ticket first.
2. The first daysThe first-month checklist says what matters: a SIM card, registration, the medical exam and the patent for a migrant, a bank account, a job or classes. The phone (bottom right) has the apps.
3. Each part of the dayPick one thing: an action on the map (work, study, rest, the market, the clinic...), a place to go by metro or bus, an app on the phone, or the 3D view to walk in. Small things at home (a tea, a shower) take no part of the day.
4. ScenesLife scenes come up with two to four choices (keys 1-9). Some cost money or give it, some change your papers, friends or family; characters remember.
5. Keep it goingKeep the needs up, pay the rent and the bills, keep papers valid (the game warns), save money, move up at work, make friends. Build the life you choose: a cook, an officer, a businessman, a student, a family.
6. The endAfter two years (or earlier) the ending screen sums up your life; a logged-in player's score goes on the online leaderboard.

3. All the features, area by area

AreaWhat the player can do
Starting a lifeChoose who you are: a Moscow "nepo baby", a regional kid, a Tajik, Uzbek or Kyrgyz labour migrant, a foreign student (from Lagos), a Dagestani student. Choose a difficulty (Easy, Normal, Realistic), make your character (skin, hair, height, clothes), and live two years (730 days).
Before Russia (foreigners)A foreigner can start at home: the passport, the invitation, the HIV test, insurance and photos, the visa form (with its typos), a consulate appointment on a calendar, the interview, collecting the visa, buying the ticket and the migration card at the border. Visa-free countries (Central Asia, the EAEU) skip the visa.
A dayEach day has three parts (morning, afternoon, evening); every action uses one. Needs fall during the day (hunger, hygiene, social contact, fun, the toilet), and energy, stress, health, money, reputation and knowledge change with what you do. Rent is paid on Saturdays. Over 4,000 life scenes come up with choices that are remembered.
Papers and the lawRegistration, the medical exam, health insurance, the work patent and its monthly payment, the language test, RVP, VNZh and citizenship, with the real state duties. Expired papers mean police checks, fines and legal strikes; three strikes mean deportation. Crime and the police: shoplifting, pickpocketing, a fight, fare dodging, drinking in public, being robbed, calling 102, witnessing, arrest, court (fines, arrest, a suspended sentence, colony) and the MVD raids that look for undocumented foreigners.
Work and moneyJob applications, shifts and promotions (Trainee to Lead), gig work (courier, barista mini-games), state and public jobs (police, traffic police, migration service, tax, customs, emergencies, bailiffs, MFC, railways, post) with a workday of decisions, the commute and parking, a business of your own, banks, cards, transfers home, loans, mortgages, savings goals, self-employment.
Home lifeRented rooms, dorms, flats, building your own house, furniture you place yourself, renovation, cleaning, the dishes, the rubbish, plants, breakdowns (a tap, a socket, the fridge, the heater), lost keys and the locksmith, the alarm clock, series, books, naps, laundry and the phone bill.
The Kitchen1,039 dishes of 28 cuisines, cooked step by step: take the ingredients from a shelf, prepare them safely (do not wash raw chicken, thaw in the fridge, boil red beans hard), answer the four steps of that kind of dish, a kitchen safety question, the doneness check and the leftovers. Stars, mistakes explained, food poisoning after dangerous ones. A drawing of every dish, suggestions, diets (halal, vegetarian, vegan), guests with allergies, the family, a fridge where food goes off, the market and haggling, equipment, ranks and unlocking, cuisine medals, a daily online challenge with a table of the best, in English and Russian.
City life437 ordinary activities in the City life app and the other apps (health, food, sport, culture, faith, friends, family and love, children, study, travel, shopping, repairs, nature, holidays in their real months, papers), each with its price, time, what it needs and what it gives.
Getting aroundThe metro of the real city, buses, trains between 37 cities, flights (a full booking site, the airport and the border), the driving school and a car, carsharing and scooters. Weather and seasons: snow, frost and rain.
PeopleFriends, dating, marriage at the ZAGS, children, family decisions, calls and money home, 14 recurring characters whose stories remember your choices, pets and stray dogs.
The phoneYour own Russian number and SIM, 20 apps: jobs, the state service, police and law, the household, the City life, the Kitchen, study, housing, daily life, trains, the shop, messages (with scam texts), dating, games, calls, the bank, and 14 online services filled in step by step.
The 3D city and the mapA 3D world where people walk, talk and work, cars that wait for people, rooms you can enter and use, billboards with players' adverts, the city hall and its weekly election; an optional real map from OpenStreetMap.
Online with other playersAccounts on the game's own server, a cloud save, the leaderboard, who is online, chat at places and in private (adults only, report and block), following and friends, homes to visit and a guest book, a city vote, adverts on the boards of the 3D city, weekly challenges, and the Kitchen's daily dish table.
Goals and endings45 achievements, a first-month checklist, goals, a journal, a hall of fame. Lives end at two years, or earlier: deported, broke, ill.
